## 1. Candidate Problem  
`20 pts`

In my role at the career center, I advise Tech students on co-op and internship searches, and repeatedly watch qualified students with rigorous project and manufacturing experience get rejected instantly by automated applicant tracking filters before any human sees their file. From what I've seen, the issue is rarely a lack of hands-on technical experience, but an inability to translate that experience into the rigid keyword structures required by corporate screening algorithms. 

---

## 2. Wickedness Test  
`25 pts`

- **No stopping rule:** You can endlessly tweak bullet points, reword verbs, and swap word phrases, but there's no clear moment where a resume is 100% done or objectively optimized for ATS.
- **Solutions are good or bad rather than true or false:** There’s no logically true or false resume, no mathematical threshold that says a resume will get you an interview or job. Applicant Tracking Systems may provide comparison scores, but they say if resumes are a good or bad match to the job description.
- **May be a symptom of another problem:** A high rejection rate at the screening stage could just be evidence of a larger structural issue of recruiting teams being buried under thousands of online applications and resorting to harsh filters keyword filters to reduce volume.
- **The problem solver is liable for consequences:** If I advise a student to rewrite their experiences around a specific angle or writing style, I would be liable for directly impacting whether they secure competitive interviews, offers, or maintain career trajectories.

**Verdict:** This is a wicked problem because everyone's hiring expectations are different, there is no single correct resume to submit for a job that can be proven to pass ATS, and intervening carries real-world consequences for student careers

---

## 3. Three Framings  
`30 pts`

**Framing A (the obvious one):** Students lack the resume writing skills required to tailor technical keywords to match automated ATS job description requirements.

**Framing B (stakeholder shift):** Recruiting teams rely on automated screening filters because they are overwhelmed by thousands of online submissions and cannot realistically review them all by hand.

**Framing C (goal shift):** The real goal is bypassing the ATS portal entirely and routing qualified talent directly to hiring managers through verified peer networks and employee referrals.

---

## 4. Commit and Justify  
`included in Framings`

**Chosen framing:** Framing B (stakeholder shift)

**Why:** Treating this strictly as a "students can't write resumes" problem ignores why the barrier exists in the first place. Corporate recruiting teams are overwhelmed with thousands of applications for single job openings and to survive the work volume, they set aggressive screening filters looking for the frequency of job description keywords. If we focus on the recruiters' workflow constraint, the real bottleneck isn't candidate incompetence, but a broken filtering workflow that confuses keyword density with actual technical capability or experience just to artificially shrink the applicant pool.

**What this framing makes invisible:** By framing this entirely around recruiter overload and subpar HR software, it hides the actionable things a student actually can control like tailoring their technical terms to the job description, cleaning up file formatting, or actively networking to get around the system entirely. It makes students look like helpless bystanders waiting on companies to fix their hiring systems instead of candidates who still have to find a way to get a job anyway.

---

## 5. Oath Check  
`15 pts`

**The tool I already assumed:** A website or app that runs a student's resume against a targeted job description, scores for ATS match compatibility, and automatically inputs missing technical keywords into the necessary bullet points.

**What would have to be true for it to be justified:** For this tool to be justified, the ATS gatekeeper would have to operate solely as a tool to determine keyword match, and recruiters must care solely about matching vocabulary phrases and technical keywords over a candidate's actual technical depth and authentic project ownership.

---

## 6. Adequacy Sketch  
`10 pts`

An adequate solution would be an ATS-readability checklist or keyword guide that lets a student verify their resume passes basic ATS systems for target job descriptions without corrupting formatting or losing technical truthfulness. We would know it reached adequacy when a student who previously faced rejections begins landing first-round automated screening calls or recruiter screenings without needing anyone to rewrite their lines for them.
